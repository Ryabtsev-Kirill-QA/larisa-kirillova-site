Biography

Larisa Nikolaevna Kirillova (née Kotova) was born on December 12, 1943, in Novosibirsk, into the family of architect N.F. Kotov. The family was evacuated to Novosibirsk from Kharkov during the Great Patriotic War and has remained there ever since, where her brother Viktor (b. 1940) and sister Olga (b. 1942) also built their families. All of N.F. Kotov’s children attended the drawing circle at the House of Pioneers of the Kirov District of Novosibirsk under the remarkable teacher Semyon Pavlovich Izhikov, who recommended that her father send Larisa to study in Leningrad — at the famous Secondary Art School (СХШ) at the Repin Institute.

In 1957 Larisa passed the entrance examinations and was accepted into the 7th grade of the Secondary Art School (now the 8th grade). In 1962 she graduated from the School and entered the Repin Institute, in the evening department of the painting faculty (it was the last enrollment for the evening department). The following year she transferred to the daytime department. She studied under V.F. Rudnev, Pen Varlen, V.V. Pimenov, P. Kiparisov, V.I. Reikhet, A.A. Debler, B.S. Ugarov, and V.M. Oreshnikov. She graduated from the Repin Institute in 1968 from the workshop of V.M. Oreshnikov.

After graduating, she worked at the Leningrad branch of the Art Fund of the RSFSR as an artist-designer from 1968 to 1971. From 1971 to 1974 she studied at the creative workshop under the guidance of V.M. Oreshnikov. From 1974 to 2014 she worked as a teacher and then as director (from 1988 to 2014) of the Secondary Art School at the Repin Institute — from 1991, the St. Petersburg State Academic Art Lyceum named after B.V. Ioganson of the Russian Academy of Arts (SPGAKhL).

She was married to A.A. Kirillov, also a painter (from 1963 to 2005). She has two daughters — Ekaterina (b. 1969) and Maria (b. 1976). From 1972 she began actively exhibiting her works at exhibitions of various levels and became involved in the public activities of the Union of Artists. In 1975 she became a member of the Leningrad branch of the Union of Artists of the USSR. She has been repeatedly honored with awards and prizes. She holds the title “Honored Artist of Russia” (1987), is a laureate of the State Prize named after I.E. Repin (1987), a corresponding member of the USSR Academy of Arts (1988), a full member of the Petrovsky Academy of Sciences and Arts (2000), and a full member of the Russian Academy of Arts (2001).
